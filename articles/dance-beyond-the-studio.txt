At first glance, dance appears to be just an art, a sport, or maybe a hobby. Often, we see little 3-year-olds in tutus, or kid/teens doing competitions. What we don't see is the adults and seniors in their 20s-60s showing up to these same-level dance classes. Why do you think they sign up for these classes? Of course, they could merely want to have fun and move their body, but dancing actually creates a 76% lower risk of dementia in senior citizens. It makes adults' risk of falling 37% smaller. This article will go over many of the other long-term effects of dance. If you're a kid/teen, adult, or senior interested in how dance can affect your body (positively and negatively), this article is for you!

The Mind

Scientists used to experiment mostly with the physical aspects of dance. As of 2008, however, they began focusing on the neurological sides of the movement as well. A neuroscientist from Columbia University found that by combining music and movement, the brain is triggered. The movement stimulates its sensory and motor circuits, and the music wakes up its reward system centers. Essentially, dance activates different aspects of the mind. Further studies have found that dancing increases serotonin, which is one of the happy hormones, and reduces stress. Dance styles like Zumba have been found to improve mood as well as cognitive thinking skills like decision-making. Along with this, dance can develop neural connections such as spatial awareness and long-term memory.

The Body

One great benefit scientists have found of dance is that it can help with Parkinson's disease. It has numerous positive effects on mobility and coordination, and these all add up to help manage Parkinson's and other body pains.
